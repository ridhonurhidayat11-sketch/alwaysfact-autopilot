Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link For many people a trip to the zoo is an opportunity to see favorite animals like gorillas, giraffes and lions. But in Alburquerque, New Mexico, U.S., visitors can add spiders, scorpions and ants to their must-see list at the ABQ BioPark BUGarium . The facility also includes an aquarium, traditional zoo and botanic garden. The BUGarium was closed for a year and reopened in October 2026 following a $1.45 million renovation. Insects are the largest group of animals on Earth, and ecologically essential as pollinators and the base of many food chains. Famed entomologist E.O. Wilson once said: “If we were to wipe out insects alone — just that group alone — on this planet, which we are trying hard to do, the rest of life and humanity with it would mostly disappear from the land. And within a few months.” The BUGarium exists to educate the public about the importance of insects. “Whether they’re pollinating plants, recycling nutrients, controlling pests, or supporting entire food webs, these animals are essential to healthy ecosystems,” said ABQ BioPark Associate Director Matthew Peterson in a statement. Bugs are not always an easy sell. “Guests come to the BUGarium with a wide spectrum of attitudes ranging from disgust and horror to fascination and awe,” Alexandra Miller, the curator of entomology at the ABQ BioPark told Mongabay in an email. “We hope guests learn how important arthropods are to ecosystems around the world, what they can do to support native populations, and that arthropods require specialized care in the same way gorillas and elephants do.” Along with spiders, scorpions, crabs and other animals with jointed exoskeletons insects are part of the taxonomic group Arthropoda. A highlight of the insect-based zoo is a leaf-cutter ant ( Atta cephalotes ) colony from a rainforest in Trinidad. It shows the complex society of ants that farm fungus, their food, on leaves that they forage. “The different chambers the ants live in are made out of blown glass, so guests have the opportunity to see how the entire ant colony operates and works cooperatively,” Miller said. The BUGarium also exhibits insects and spiders from New Mexico’s Chihuahuan Desert. “Connecting our guests to nature is a cornerstone of what we do at the BioPark, and at the BUGarium our focus is creating meaningful connections between guests and arthropods,” Miller said. Other U.S. zoos like the San Diego Zoo and Cincinnati Zoo also have dedicated exhibits for connecting people with insects. However, insect and spider exhibits are not nearly as common as exhibits of popular vertebrate species. “The BUGarium is part of a small group of facilities dedicated to live insects and arthropods in the United States. Our numbers may be small, but like the animals in our care, can have a huge impact,” Miller said. “We are proud to be a part of the story of connecting people to arthropods and promoting their conservation.” Banner image : Leaf-cutter ants at ABQ BioPark BUGarium.
