The company notched the milestone on Saturday (Nov. 14), when the launch tower at its Starbase site in South Texas caught Starship's Ship spacecraft with its "chopstick" arms. You've probably seen photos and videos showing the epic snag from afar. But on Tuesday (Nov. 17), SpaceX released up-close views — a series of four slo-mo videos captured by cameras near the top of the 480-foot-tall (146 meters) tower. The Ship upper stage of SpaceX's Starship megarocket comes back to its launch tower for a "chopsticks" catch on Nov. 14, 2026, during the vehicle's 15th test flight. (Image credit: SpaceX) "The Starbase tower chopsticks catching their first Starship. This unique architecture was designed for full and rapid reusability, enabling repeat launches and landings from the same pad," SpaceX wrote in a Tuesday X post that shared the videos, a compilation of which you can see embedded above. Starship is the biggest and most powerful rocket ever built. Its current "V3" version stands about 408 feet (124 m) tall, and future iterations will be even larger .
