The spacecraft's mission included the study of Mars and the Martian moon Phobos, with a planned close approach and deployment of landing probes, and over the next several months, the spacecraft successfully captured more than three dozen images of Phobos during its time in service. However, early in the following year, the spacecraft's mission abruptly ended after the Phobos 2 ground team mysteriously lost contact with the spacecraft. Adding to the mystery were objects in some of the final photographs dispatched by the doomed spacecraft, which seemed to show an elongated shadow beneath the probe — a strange twist in one of the Soviet Union's final space missions, and one that soon gave rise to conspiracy theories about what the fate of Phobos 2 might have been. So what caused the odd-looking appearance of the shadow in the final images captured by Phobos 2, and what was the real reason behind the spacecraft's sudden failure? Last of the Soviet space explorers Originally, the Soviet Union had planned for Phobos 2 to reach the Red Planet by late January 1989, after which the spacecraft would begin collecting data about the Martian environment leading up to its initial contact with Mars' largest moon in early June. A model of one of the Phobos spacecraft on display in the Memorial Museum of Cosmonautics in Moscow, Russia.
