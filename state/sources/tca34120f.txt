The morning of the launch (Nov. 13), SpaceX invited media on a tour of Starbase's Starfactory facility, where it manufactures and assembles both stages of its massive Starship rocket - an unprecedented behind-the-scenes look at the production and scale of SpaceX's operations, and a glimpse of things to come as Starship notches more and more milestones the more it flies. For some context, the five-story-tall Starfactory spans approximately 1 million square feet (~93,000 meters), with operations split between Starship's Super Heavy boosters and upper stage Ships, and nearly 330,000 additional square feet (30,000 square meters) of office space. SpaceX officials describe its design as one more akin to an automotive manufacturing plant than that of a typical aerospace production site, and inside, it is absolutely alive with activity. Inside Starfactory We entered through an arch-covered doorway underneath the giant "X" sign on the building's shorter face. Our media gaggle was met with curious glances as we were shown through the doors and guided onto the factory floor - few are ever able to cross that threshold outside of SpaceX employees, and our presence seemed as novel to those busy working as it was to us. Inside, we were struck with the sheer enormity of Starship's scale. Giant stainless-steel rocket parts were meticulously scattered across different subassembly stations throughout the building, with Ship nose cones, cylindrical sections and booster components all under various stages of manufacture. The sheer quantity of the hardware was staggering, and being so close to each as we walked Starfactory's throughways was an eye-opener.
