(Image credit: CSA/Joshua Kutryk) The Himalayas tower below the clouds, capped in icy snow in new snapshots captured from aboard the International Space Station . What is it? Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk was looking out one of the windows aboard the space station when the most incredible view came into focus — and thankfully he had a camera handy and was able to capture these striking scenes of the Himalayas from space. Above the mountain range, fluffy clouds hang in the atmosphere. Below, the expansive Himalayas are covered in bright snow and ice, which looks somehow both white and blue at the same time as the bright sunlight streaks across the breathtaking expanse. Latest Videos From Space View more Watch full video here: This frosty winter scene showcases the mountain range from orbit, from about 250 miles (400 kilometers) above Earth's surface. But even from this distance, the magnitude of this mountain range is palpable. The Himalayas stretch across five different countries including Nepal, India, China, Bhutan and Pakistan. And the Himalayas include some of the highest mountain peaks on planet Earth, including Mount Everest. Himalayas 🗻 https://t.co/IzGzu20H4Q November 17, 2026 Why is it incredible? From orbit around Earth, you might expect even the largest landmarks on our planet to appear miniscule. But somehow, in these photographs snapped by Kutryk during NASA's SpaceX Crew-13 mission , even from hundreds of miles above our planet you can really see and feel the immense scale of this mammoth mountain range.
