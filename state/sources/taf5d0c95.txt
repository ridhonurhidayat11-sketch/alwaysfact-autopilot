Robert Web Editor Nov 18, 2026 Article Feet in woolen socks by the fireplace. Holidays are often a fun break during a busy year – a long weekend here and there is an event many of us can get behind. However, when a short holiday break turns into a full holiday season, we know that this is a different “ball game”. Everyone has stress about something – some people love the holidays, and some people are holiday-avoidant… and some live in the middle. What specific stressors or challenges you experience might not bother your sibling, your spouse or your coworker. We want to be aware of and have a clear understanding of what holidays mean to you as a person. The first step is to acknowledge what your feelings and emotions are – you may enjoy seeing the neighborhood holiday lights but not want to decorate yourself (totally okay!!), you may love shopping for the perfect gift for everyone on your list, you may avoid all of the activities — everyone one of these is an okay choice… you can be yourself always in all ways. Give yourself that permission. As you prepare, keep a list or a calendar of events – expected/obligated, okay to go or not go, Holiday Calendar: Expected/ Obligated Need to do Would be fun to do Nope Family dinner Work party off-site Travel Host at my house Religious services Neighborhood party Shopping Restaurants Combine this with your standard calendar and make a schedule. If you want, add to the list what your part is (bring a dish, gift, coordinate a visit, etc.). Having the majority of activities written down will help you not overbook, help you plan ahead, make it easier to say no, spend within your budget. Before you commit to something, say “I have to check my calendar first…. I will get back to you”. Prepare your budget – We often budget for gifts but forget that we will be buying more groceries for those special meals and favorite dishes…. We also need to budget for travel, having guests in your home, clothing, even allow for a higher electric bill. Write it down – keep receipts – let your immediate family know that this is what we have to spend, then track it as you go. Now that you have a current calendar and a working budget, set realistic expectations for your holiday experience. We know that not everything is optional and traditions will continue but none of it has to be perfect…minor stress and family friction can be normal. When telling someone no, use “I” statements and stick to the single issue at hand. In addition to your list/calendar, remember to prioritize time for yourself. Keep your regular sleep schedule as much as possible, eat well (if you have a heavy meal one day, lighten up the next), schedule time to get fresh air and a quick break. ALWAYS focus on what matters.
