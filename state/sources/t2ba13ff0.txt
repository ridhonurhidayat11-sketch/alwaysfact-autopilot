Recreational Fishing Data: A Message from NOAA's Laura Grimm November 19, 2026 Laura Grimm, NOAA Deputy Under Secretary for Oceans and Atmosphere, provides an update on the Recreational Angler Partnership Improvement Directive and how it is advancing the Restoring American Saltwater Angling and Recreation Executive Order. Leadership Message | Alaska New England/Mid-Atlantic Pacific Islands Southeast West Coast National Families enjoy a day of fishing in the Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary. Credit: NOAA Fisheries Marine recreational fishing remains a key component of our nation's economy and an economic engine for coastal communities across the country. The recent 2025 Status of the Stocks report highlighted an all-time high of 96 percent of our U.S. fish stocks are not subject to overfishing. In 2025, we removed Atlantic blue marlin, Gulf of America gag grouper, and Mid-Atlantic summer flounder from the overfishing list, among other species. NOAA continues to work on behalf of both recreational and commercial fishermen to ensure U.S. fisheries are the most sustainable in the world. The economic impact of the recreational fishing industry remains very strong. The 2024 Fisheries Economics of the United States report once again highlighted the importance of marine recreational fishing across our coastal communities.
