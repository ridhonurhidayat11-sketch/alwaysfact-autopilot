Abstract Current state-of-the-art peptide synthesis faces a significant global challenge in efficiency and sustainability. 1 With demand for peptide therapeutics growing rapidly, 2,3 our ability to provide these important medicines is under increasing pressure. 4 Chemical synthesis typically proceeds C-to-N and requires protecting groups, whereas ribosomal synthesis proceeds N-to-C from unprotected amino acids (AAs). To improve efficiency and sustainability, we developed a continuous N-to-C Peptide Synthesis (NCPS) using N-/C-unprotected AAs or fragments without intermediate isolations, achieving ≥95% average yield per coupling and ≤1.0% epimerization. The method enabled seven consecutive couplings. Starting from N-/C-unprotected AAs, peptide assembly requires fewer isolations than those needed to prepare the corresponding Fmoc-AA building blocks before conventional synthesis can begin. This method accepts all 20 canonical AAs and a wide range of non-canonical AAs important for drug-like profiles. 5 Longer peptides can be readily assembled through continuous fragment coupling while maintaining chiral integrity. This approach reduces isolations by ~10-fold and waste generation by up to ~100-fold. We further developed a flow synthesizer that enhances speed and productivity while mitigating batch manufacturing risks. The method enabled the synthesis of a tirzepatide heptapeptide fragment, degarelix, a 14-mer engineered macrocyclic peptide, and semaglutide. Overall, continuous NCPS has the potential to transform peptide manufacturing.
