The robotic Progress 97 cargo craft launched atop a Soyuz rocket from the Russia-run Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan today (Nov. 24) at 5:17 a.m. EST (1013 GMT; 3:17 p.m. Baikonur time), carrying several tons of supplies for the astronauts aboard the orbiting lab. If all goes to plan, Progress 97 will arrive at the International Space Station (ISS) on Thursday (Nov. 26), docking with its Poisk module. But the freighter won't stay at that particular locale for long. A Russian Soyuz rocket launches the uncrewed Progress 97 cargo ship to the International Space Station from Baikonur Cosmodrome, Kazakhstan on Nov. 24, 2026. (Image credit: NASA) The cosmonauts currently living on the ISS — Pyotr Dubrov, Anna Kikina and Sergey Teteryatnikov — will unload Progress 97 through Poisk. But the freighter will then hop over to a different port, according to journalist and space historian Anatoly Zak: the aft one of the PrK transfer tunnel, which leads to Russia's Zvezda service module. The PrK port is the best site for engine burns that boost the orbit of the ISS, a service that Progress freighters regularly provide. But Progress 97 cannot unload its cargo through that port because the PrK transfer tunnel has been sealed off, due to an ongoing air leak that was first documented in 2019. Progress-97 is therefore "the first cargo mission scheduled to unload its cargo and perform orbit corrections of the ISS at different docking ports," Zak wrote on the site he runs, RussianSpaceWeb.com.
