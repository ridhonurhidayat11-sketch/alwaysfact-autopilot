How Super Mario explains Renaissance art A step or a jump Alex Bellos Mon 23 Nov 2026 02.10 EST Last modified on Mon 23 Nov 2026 03.04 EST Share Prefer the Guardian on Google Today’s puzzle is about the hidden mathematical pattern behind a much-loved video game. Consider a simplified game of Super Mario. He moves from platform to platform. He can either step forward 1 space, to the next platform, or jump forward 2 spaces, to the platform after that. He starts on the first platform. How many ways are there for Mario to land on the last platform if the level has: 3 platforms? 4 platforms? 5 platforms? 10 platforms? And what has this got to do with Leonardo da Vinci? The point here is not to do too much calculation. Spot the pattern, and you should be able to easily calculate how many ways to cross any reasonable number of platforms. I’ll be back at 5pm UK with the solution. PLEASE NO SPOILERS Instead discuss classic video games and Renaissance art. Today’s puzzle was suggested by Dr Tom Crawford, who is an Oxford don , a successful maths Youtuber , and a lover of science fiction and fantasy. His new book How Many Pikachus Would it Take to Power a Lightbulb? asks entertaining questions about fantasy worlds and answers them with proper maths. Such as using the Navier-Stokes equations to reveal the aerodymics of Quidditch broomsticks, working out the half-life of the Incredible Hulk and calculating who is richer, Sonic the Hedgehog or Super Mario, based on the gold they have collected. How Many Pikachus Would it Take to Power a Lightbulb? The Real Maths of Fantasy Worlds by Tom Crawford is available at the Guardian Bookshop.
