View author publications Search author on: PubMed Google Scholar Email Bluesky Facebook LinkedIn Reddit Whatsapp X Save article View saved research Female mosquitoes use several sensory systems to locate a host whose blood they can feed on. One of these systems is a group of proteins known as ionotropic receptors, which detect the acidic compounds in human sweat that contribute to its sour smell. Writing in Nature , Xu et al. 1 report 3D structures of an ionotropic-receptor complex from the malaria-spreading mosquito Anopheles stephensi . The structures show how the binding of an acid activates the receptor, which then opens its ion-conducting pore and generates an electrical signal that helps to guide the mosquito towards a human host.
