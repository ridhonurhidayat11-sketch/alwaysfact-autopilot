Taveau Nov 18, 2026 MEDIA ADVISORY M26-089 NASA Headquarters NASA’s Northrop Grumman Commercial Resupply Services 24 mission launched Saturday, April 11, 2026, aboard the company’s Cygnus XL spacecraft atop a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. NASA’s Northrop Grumman Commercial Resupply Services 25 mission will deliver approximately 11,000 pounds of science and supplies to the International Space Station. Credit: Northrop Grumman NASA, Northrop Grumman, and SpaceX are targeting no earlier than 10:47 a.m. EST, Sunday, Nov. 29, for the next launch delivering science investigations, supplies, and equipment to the International Space Station. The agency also will hold a prelaunch media teleconference on Monday, Nov. 23. Filled with approximately 11,000 pounds of cargo, the Northrop Grumman Cygnus XL spacecraft, aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket, will launch from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. The mission is known as NASA’s Northrop Grumman Commercial Resupply Services 25, or Northrop Grumman CRS-25. After arriving at the International Space Station, NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, who are living and working aboard the space station, will use the Canadarm2 robotic arm to capture the Cygnus XL on Tuesday, Dec. 1. Ground controllers then will install it to the Unity module’s Earth-facing port for cargo unloading. NASA’s live launch and docking coverage will stream through a variety of platforms. Learn where to watch online: https://www.nasa.gov/live Highlights of space station research and technology demonstrations being delivered aboard this Cygnus XL spacecraft include experiments to improve drug delivery for cancer and osteoarthritis, predict cancer spread, and advance treatments for muscle loss, as well as improve radiation sensing and advance food production for future long-duration missions. The spacecraft is scheduled to remain at the orbiting laboratory until May 2027, then depart with several thousand pounds of disposal items and other unneeded cargo, burning up harmlessly during re-entry. Northrop Grumman named the spacecraft the S.S. James “Jim” A. Lovell in honor of the former NASA astronaut. Lovell flew four missions, including Gemini VII, Gemini XII, Apollo 8, and the Apollo 13 mission around the Moon.
