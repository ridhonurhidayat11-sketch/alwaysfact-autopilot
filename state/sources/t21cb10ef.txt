The new show casts replicants as the dominant species on Earth, with humans relegated to second-class citizens after a global uprising. This dreary dystopian cyberpunk realm originates from sci-fi legend Phillip K. Dick's 1968 novel, "Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?," and was then so vividly brought to life in Ridley Scott’s seminal 1982 adaptation, " Blade Runner ." The 2017 sequel, "Blade Runner 2049," was directed by "Arrival’s" and "Dune’s" Denis Villeneuve. Starring Michelle Yeoh, Hunter Schafer, Matthew Needham, Dimitri Abold, and Lewis Gribben, " Blade Runner 2099 " hails from creator and showrunner Silka Louisa and Alcon Entertainment founders Andrew Kosove and Broderick Johnson, who executive produce with Ridley Scott's Scott Free Productions. Here, Yeoh's dying replicant blade runner Olwen unites with Schafer’s Cora, a human masquerading as a synthetic, to hunt down a dangerous rogue replicant that holds the key to a dark secret. We spoke to Kosove and Johnson about being respectful keepers of "Blade Runner's" creative flame, the pitch that sold them, and dipping back into Dick's original novel.
