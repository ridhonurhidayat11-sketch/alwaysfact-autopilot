In September 2026 I recorded 82 fatal landslides causing 173 fatalities . This year continues to show a higher than average trend in fatal landslide occurrence. This is my regular update for the number of fatal global landslides, focusing on September 2026. As usual, this data has been collected in line with the methodology described in Froude and Petley (2018) and in Petley (2012) . References are listed below – please cite these articles if you use this analysis. Data presented in these updates should be treated as being provisional at this stage as I will reanalyse them prior to formal publication, and other events will emerge. Note that this data excludes landslides triggered by earthquakes. The headline figures are as follows: September 2026: 82 fatal landslides causing 173 fatalities . This is the number of landslides by month in 2026 to the end of September:- The number of global fatal landslides in 2026 by month to the end of September. The September data is interesting – 82 fatal landslides is the second highest monthly total that I have recorded, beaten only by 2024. The 2004-2016 mean is just 39.6. However, the number of fatalities was low, primarily because most of the fatal events recorded involved only a single fatality. There were two events that each claimed 13 lives My preferred way of presenting this data is to use the cumulative total by pentad. This graph is up to pentad 55, which captures almost all of the September data (it runs to 2 October):- The cumulative total number of global fatal landslides in 2026 by pentad to the end of September. Whilst 2026 is not on a par with the exceptional year of 2024 in terms of the number of fatalities, the degree to which it is exceeding the long term mean is clear. Overall, the pattern of fatal landslides for 2026 is atypical, with peaks in July and September, but a much lower total in August. This is most likely to be associated with the pattern of the South Asian monsoon, but it requires more research. As we move into the northern hemisphere Autumn, the impacts of the very large El Niño should start to become apparent. It is hard to forecast what might happen. The latest (12 November) report indicates that the El Niño continues to strengthen, and is likely to continue to do so until early 2027 . References Froude, M. and Petley, D.N. 2018.
