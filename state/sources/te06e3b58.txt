Even before its launch, GTA VI has already become a global phenomenon . Its first trailer, released in December 2023, attracted more than 90 million YouTube views within 24 hours . It broke the Guinness World Record for the most-viewed non-music video in 24 hours, while sparking fan theories, online debates and a wave of social media content that kept the hype alive. In GTA VI, players will follow Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a criminal duo whose story unfolds in Vice City, a fictional city inspired by Miami, and the fictional state of Leonida, based on Florida. The game is expected to explore familiar GTA territory, including drugs, gang rivalries and organised crime. Why are millions of people so excited about a game that has taken more than a decade to make? Read more: How GTA 6 became a global phenomenon we can’t ignore The game everyone is waiting for Since its debut in 1997, the GTA franchise has evolved through several generations of gaming consoles, selling more than 475 million copies worldwide . Because of its depiction of violence , GTA has always been controversial. The debate intensified when the games were cited as an influence in a case involving an eight-year-old boy in Louisiana who fatally shot his grandmother. Its adult themes and sexually explicit content are likely to spark controversy again when GTA VI arrives On the technical side, Rockstar, the studio behind the franchise, helped redefine gaming with its open-world “sandbox” design. Players could explore freely and take part in activities that resemble everyday life . The earliest games focused mainly on car chases, shootouts and destruction, viewed from above. GTA III changed the formula by introducing a fully 3D open world. Later titles expanded player freedom even further, allowing them to buy properties, run businesses and explore beyond the main storyline. As the successor to GTA V, widely regarded as a benchmark for open-world gaming, GTA VI faces enormous pressure to raise the bar. Two delays have only fuelled players’ curiosity , suggesting that Rockstar wants more time to get the details right. In a way, waiting has become part of the fun. This reflects an idea from economist George Loewenstein : looking forward to something can itself be a source of enjoyment. Rockstar’s marketing strategy also echoes marketing professor Stephen Brown’s argument that brands can build excitement by teasing customers and keeping them waiting. Nostalgia adds another layer. Many players who first explored GTA: Vice City in 2002 are now adults, eager to revisit its neon-lit streets. For them, GTA VI is not just a new game but a return to a familiar virtual world. Read more: Grand Theft Auto: players are voting, debating, protesting and making political decisions With great expectations comes great pressure to deliver Once GTA VI hits the shelves, one big question will remain: can it live up to years of hype and sky-high expectations? The excitement has already fuelled a massive wave of user-generated content, from YouTube trailer reactions (the official trailer has surpassed 300 million views , alongside more than a million comments) to Reddit discussions , fan theories and memes across social media. GTA VI is also projected to attract 15 million digital pre-orders across PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, potentially generating US$1.4 billion. If realised, this would set a new pre-order record. The figures point to strong brand attachment , with millions of fans willing to invest their time, money and patience before the game is even released. By the time players return to Vice City, many will already feel familiar with its streets. But whether they enjoy the game will depend not only on its quality, but also on how well it matches their expectations. This is known as expectation-disconfirmation theory : the higher the expectations, the harder it is for a product to satisfy consumers. The gaming industry has seen what happens when a major release falls short. Cyberpunk 2077, one of the most anticipated games of 2020, launched with widespread technical problems. Sony removed it from the PlayStation Store and offered refunds after the game ran poorly on the PS4 and Xbox One. For Rockstar, the hype surrounding GTA VI is both an opportunity and a risk. The game could become another landmark release, but failing to meet expectations could trigger an equally massive backlash. After all, this is a blockbuster reportedly costing up to US$2 billion to develop and market. Read more: Grand Theft Auto 6 could make US$3–5 billion in the next week. Here’s how games make so much money A new era for gaming consoles? GTA VI promises a technically ambitious gaming experience, including a highly dynamic weather system that mirrors the real world . But it is expected to run at only 30fps for both consoles, even though modern games run at 60fps. Does this mean we need a new generation of gaming consoles console? From a business standpoint, however, GTA VI’s release does not necessarily mean a new generation of gaming consoles is just around the corner. A study of the US console market from 1993 to 2004 by marketing researchers Jeroen Binken and Stefan Stremersch found that the release of a “superstar” game could increase hardware sales by an average of 14% during the launch month and the following five months. Both Sony (with the PS6) and Microsoft (with Xbox Helix) have confirmed that next-generation consoles are in development. However, firm release dates remain unclear. The industry is also facing a global chip supply shortage driven partly by growing demand from data centres, artificial intelligence and electric vehicles. More broadly, GTA VI highlights how major game publishers manage financial risks. In his research on the blockbuster “AAA” gaming industry, David Nieborg , a media studies scholar at the University of Toronto Scarborough, explains why studios increasingly rely on established franchises to reduce uncertainty in an unpredictable market. GTA VI shows just how powerful an established brand can be. Beyond the hype surrounding its release, the phenomenon reveals something about modern consumer behaviour: consumption does not always begin when we buy or use a product. Sometimes, it starts much earlier, when we begin looking forward to it.
