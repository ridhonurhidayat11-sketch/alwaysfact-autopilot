Some of that material now hangs on a gallery wall in New York City, embedded in artist Ashley Zelinskie's print of the very explosion it survived. Look closely at the print and you can see little flakes on the surface. Some of Zelinskie's friends vacationing on the islands had come across pieces of heat-shield tile. "I think you might want these," Zelinskie recalled one friend telling her. She did. The Brooklyn-based artist ground the fragments into dust and worked them into the print. Ashley Zelinskie incorporated dust from recovered SpaceX Starship heat-shield tiles into this print of the vehicle breaking apart over Turks and Caicos. (Image credit: Ashley Zelinskie) The Starship print is part of "Ejecta," which captures rocket plumes and explosions on paper. It's one of four series in Zelinskie's solo exhibition, "The Weight of Space," which opened Nov. 20 at Heft Gallery in Manhattan. Her marble sculptures in "Monuments" depict the Hubble Space Telescope, retired NASA astronaut Mike Massimino's spaceflight gear and more. Five "Launch Sequence" sculptures follow a rocket through liftoff, and the interactive "Crowded Sky" lets visitors track what's currently orbiting overhead in real time. Zelinskie gave Space.com Editor-in-Chief Tariq Malik and me a tour before the gallery doors opened. Massimino joined her that evening to celebrate the exhibition and their collaboration on "Monuments." You can watch the tour in the video above. Monuments Massimino turned up in our first look at Zelinskie's work, too. In 2022, I photographed the two of them beside her "Exploration JWST" sculpture at "Unfolding the Universe: First Light," an exhibit inspired by the James Webb Space Telescope's first images.
