Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link Mongabay Kids has launched Wild Guardians , a free multimedia educational series that connects upper elementary students with nature and inspires them to become stewards of wild things and wild places. Wild Guardians features stories of children and young people around the world working to protect their favorite animals and ecosystems. Through fun five-minute-long videos, illustrated comics and standards-aligned lessons and activities for fourth and fifth graders, Wild Guardians aims to bridge the gap between storytelling and environmental stewardship. The series features three different conservation topics: Restoring habitat for giraffes in Tanzania, the colorful world of wild bees and exploring and protecting waterways in Tennessee, U.S. Here are the adventures Wild Guardians takes children on: Growing food for giants Follow a group of primary school students in Tanzania who are growing food for giraffes and their community. Children will learn about how they can support their own local wildlife through tree planting, cleanups and habitat restoration. Exploring and protecting waterways Elementary students in Chattanooga, Tennessee, explore their local creek and watershed, home to wildlife such as the hellbender and crawfish. Children will learn that waterways connect landscapes, from mountains to sea, how human activities affect freshwater ecosystems and what they can do to help care for local waterways and the wildlife that depend on them. Discovering wild bees Students learn that there are more 20,000 species of bees, most of them wild bees that don’t produce honey. Children will go on a wild bee safari to learn about the importance of wild bees for pollination, how their habitat is being fragmented and what they can do to help. “The great thing about the Wild Guardians resources is that students are learning about conservation in their local community alongside math, English language arts and social studies,” said Wild Guardians education lead Emily Townsend. “Wild Guardians resources are versatile. The toolkits each focus on a different conservation topic and include a video, comic and interdisciplinary lessons. Teachers choose what works best for their classroom and all the resources are included to engage students as stewards of their community,” Townsend said. “One of the things I find most meaningful about Wild Guardians is its potential to help children move from simply learning about wildlife and ecosystems to understanding that they can play an active role in protecting them. Children need opportunities to connect what they learn in the classroom with the places, species and environmental challenges around them,” said Mallory Lindsay of The Chuck & Ernestina Kreutzkamp Foundation’s Earth Focus Initiative, the primary funder of the Wild Guardians project. “The Wild Guardians project provides a practical toolkit for kids to take action for nature. This gives kids a sense of hope,” said Veila Makundi and Monica Bond of the Wild Nature Institute, whose work is featured in the video about children in Tanzania growing food for giraffes and their community.
