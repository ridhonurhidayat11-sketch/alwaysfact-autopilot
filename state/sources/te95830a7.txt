Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link Around 40 community representatives gathered early in the morning on Nov. 24 at a county assembly hall in Lamu, a town on an island off Kenya’s coast, to virtually witness a court hearing. The case pits residents against the Dangote group, owned by Nigerian billionaire Aliko Dangote. The conglomerate plans to build an oil refinery on lands where the residents claim they have lived for generations. With an estimated cost of $16 billion and a reported production capacity of 700,000 barrels per day , the East Africa Petroleum Refinery, if it goes ahead, would be the largest in Africa. However, some residents appear not to have been informed of the project beforehand. Because they do not hold formal land title deeds, the government is asking them to leave, the community representatives said. “We want information,” Mzee Hilal Suleiman Ali, an elder from the indigenous Mvinjeni community who attended the hearing, told Mongabay. “We are not opposed to the project, but we want to be included, and above all, we want to keep our land.” On Sept. 30, Dangote held a groundbreaking ceremony attended by several African heads of state. The November hearing was held at the Environment and Land Court in Malindi, around 200 kilometers (125 miles) from Lamu along the Kenyan coast. Because traveling to attend in person would be too expensive, the communities witnessed the proceedings remotely using a phone. Their lawyer, who was present with them, participated in the hearing virtually. After several hours, the judge announced an adjournment on procedural grounds. “We would prefer to adjourn the hearing until Dec. 15, by which time you will have brought us the documents,” the presiding judge, Jane Onyango, told the plaintiff’s lawyer. As the complaint was filed by email, the judge required that it be submitted in person in Nairobi, with copies for all the parties concerned, before the case can proceed. In the room, one could feel the frustration among the community representatives growing. “It seems like they are trying to delay the process and discourage us from filing a complaint,” Daniel Mwaniki Mwangi, a farmer and resident of Lamu county, told Mongabay. “The government should not abandon its people. We have filed a court case, we will not abandon it, and we will follow through with it.” George Wakahiu, the plaintiffs’ lawyer, remained optimistic about the situation. “It is nothing. I’ll go to Nairobi and do what they are asking for,” he said, adding that: “[T]he National Land Commission is now coming to the ground to see who is affected. They have now agreed to talk to the people. Before, they refused to talk to the people, so I am optimistic that there will be a discussion and everything will be sorted out.” Banner image: George Wakahiu, the lawyer representing community members, pleading remotely before the Environment and Land Court in Malindi, seen here alongside members of the affected communities.
