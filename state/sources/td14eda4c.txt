Campaigners fear the proposed centre at Keswick, on the edge of Norwich, will create noise pollution and have a negative impact on nearby Marston Marsh. Smith, honorary life president and former majority shareholder of Norwich City FC, said: "I love Norfolk. You can't be involved in a football club for 55 years without being attached to the people and the beauty of Norfolk. I feel very strongly about it." But developer Norwich Apex said an ecological impact assessment concluded no effect on the reserve was expected. Image source, Shaun Whitmore/BBC Image caption, Delia Smith, pictured with fellow campaigner Jake Humphrey, says she feels very strongly about protecting Norfolk Data centres are facilities that store and process vast amounts of data and run the software and online services used by millions of people. This centre, off the A140 Ipswich Road, would need 150 MW of power to run and would feature 72 chiller units on its roof. According to planning documents, the building would be about 20m (65ft) high. A planning application for the centre has been lodged with South Norfolk Council. If approved, construction could begin at the end of 2027 and it would be operational by 2028. The plans have emerged at the same time as a much bigger proposal for a data centre and solar farm elsewhere in Norfolk, south of King's Lynn, which would require more than five times as much electricity. What are data centres and are more coming to the East? Published 17 November 2025 The developer has said the Keswick facility would use " air-cooling technology " and not require water for cooling. But Smith, who lives in Suffolk, said: "We haven't got enough water, and data centres use an awful lot of water. ""It's one of the biggest [data centres] and It doesn't do anything for the Norfolk community. Where is the £1.2bn coming from? And who are the people who are going to presumably make money out of it? "It's not going to do anything for the local community, it's the opposite. It's going to be bad." Image source, Shaun Whitmore/BBC Image caption, Campaigners have raised concerns about the proposed centre's effect on the nearby Marston Marsh nature reserve It is hoped the centre will harness wind energy by connecting to the Hornsea 3 substation nearby. Smith said: "Renewable energy is going into the grid but it doesn't supply everything now. "What are you going to do when you have this huge data centre absolutely sucking up energy?" Other opponents of the plan include broadcaster and entrepreneur Jake Humphrey . Image source, Shaun Whitmore/BBC Image caption, Matt Tomlinson is worried that nocturnal wildlife including owls and bats will be driven away from the area Other campaigners include Matt Tomlinson of Yare Valley Meadow Makers, who believes the development will have a "disastrous effect" on nocturnal wildlife. "On this Marston Marsh site we have nine protected bat species, we have three different owl species and these will all need the quiet conditions to effectively hunt," he said. "I think this is an awful idea; awful for the wildlife and awful for the people." In recent days, Labour MP for South Norfolk, Ben Goldsborough, who believes the development would be in the wrong place , has been appointed to a new data centre committee that will scrutinise the government's national policies. Matt Bartram, chief executive of Norwich Apex, said: "Our ambition is to deliver one of the UK's greenest data centres, using low-carbon power supplied via Norwich Main substation. Protecting nearby ecological sites, including Marston Marshes has shaped the design from the outset. "Detailed environment assessments have informed our plans to reduce noise and screen the development from view. "We propose planting more than 15,700 trees and over 1.7km of new hedgerow, with the plans delivering a 13% biodiversity net gain." Correction 24 November 2026: This article was amended to clarify the size of this data centre in comparison to other planned developments and to make clear the developer's position on water usage.
