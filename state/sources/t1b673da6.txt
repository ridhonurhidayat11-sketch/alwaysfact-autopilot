After "The Wrath of Khan" and "The Search for Spock" went to some pretty dark places — ear worms, the destruction of the Enterprise, the death (and later resurrection) of a certain beloved Vulcan — the franchise was in need of some light relief. So what better than to set coordinates for the then-present day of 1986 San Francisco, for a time-travel comedy romp? Spock himself, Leonard Nimoy, had directed "The Search for Spock" and Paramount had lined him up to call the shots on a follow-up before the movie had even landed in theaters. It was a massive shift away from Khan and Kruge, the grandstanding villains in movies II and III. Nimoy also wanted the film to have an environmental message and appeal beyond the franchise's traditional fanbase. The film would actually be promoted with minimal "Trek" branding in some territories.
