The veteran Hayabusa2 spacecraft launched back in 2014 to collect material from the asteroid Ryugu . Since delivering the samples to Earth in December 2020 via a reentry capsule, the main spacecraft has been conducting an extended mission of exploration and in July made a super-close flyby of the two-headed asteroid Torifune. But Hayabusa2's continued adventures are now set for a premature end. The spacecraft was scheduled to rendezvous with, and attempt to land on, the tiny, 36-foot-wide (11 meters) near-Earth asteroid 1998 KY26 in 2031, but trouble with the aging spacecraft's engines has forced a change of plan. But Hayabusa2's continued adventures are now set for a premature end. The spacecraft was scheduled to rendezvous with, and attempt to land on, the tiny, 36-foot-wide (11 meters) near-Earth asteroid 1998 KY26 in 2031, but trouble with the aging spacecraft's engines has forced a change of plan. The spacecraft will instead target 2015 BM510, another tiny asteroid. And there will be no landing: Hayabusa2 is scheduled to make a high-speed flyby of the near-Earth object in April 2028.
