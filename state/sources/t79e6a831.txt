Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link Zambia’s top court has postponed for the fourth time an appeal by copper miner Sino Metals, which is trying to block a lawsuit against it from proceeding. The latest adjournment comes amid ongoing concerns over disputed resettlement offers. In February 2025, the dam holding the mining waste at the Sino Metals Leach Zambia Limited mine near the community of Kalusale, in Copperbelt province, collapsed. This released millions of liters of acidic mining waste, contaminating soils, crops, and waterways connected to the Kafue River system, which millions of Zambians rely on. In response, 176 people from Kalusale and Chambishi filed a constitutional petition in the Lusaka High Court in September 2025, seeking compensation for the damage caused by the spill. They also demanded environmental restoration and support for affected households, arguing their constitutional rights had been violated. Sino Metals appealed to the court to have the petition thrown out, arguing that a constitutional petition was not the correct legal procedure to bring the claim. It asserted that Zambia’s Bill of Rights does not apply to private corporations, only to the state. The High Court dismissed these objections, so Sino Metals took its appeal to the Supreme Court. While hearing the company’s appeal in June 2026, the Supreme Court allowed the Zambian government to join the case as the matter affected the wider public. The latest Supreme Court adjournment, on Nov. 18, follows an affidavit submitted by Attorney General Mulilo Dimas Kabesha, who asked the court to give the parties additional time to negotiate a settlement. “The Government of the Republic of Zambia is actively working with the parties to [resolve] this matter and other relevant stakeholders with a view of achieving a comprehensive and holistic resolution of all issues arising from the incident and not merely those affecting the litigants before this Honourable Court,” Kabesha wrote in the affidavit, which Mongabay reviewed. The latest adjournment comes as some residents involved in the court case say they’re being pressured to move to a new settlement, despite opposing the proposed relocation. Community members involved in the lawsuit have rejected multiple settlement proposals offered by Sino Metals. Residents told Mongabay the company has begun relocating residents to Mutenda, approximately 90 kilometers (56 miles) from the affected community of Kalusale. A Kalusale resident, who asked not to be identified for safety reasons, told Mongabay the company started demolishing the houses of those who had moved. “[The company and government] turn a blind eye to what is happening as though they do not know that we’ve got an active court case in the Supreme Court,” the resident said. In his affidavit, Kabesha argued that “substantial progress” had already been made, referring to a resettlement plan launched by Sino Metals in September 2026. The next hearing in the case is scheduled for January 2027. Banner image of Kausale, the community affected by the tailings dam spill. Image courtesy of Anonymous.
