"We were actually looking for planets ," Étienne Artigau of the Trottier Institute for Research on Exoplanets at the Université de Montréal, who led the discovery, said in a statement . "Instead, we found something much more surprising." So surprising that there are no similar systems within at least 100 light-years . Furthermore, the configuration of the system seems impossible. "This system should not exist," added Montreal's Patrick Dufour. "We have no clean explanation for how it got here." This triple system has been called Welopō'ai, which is a portmanteau of two Hawaiian words that means a 'path of family traits', and to understand the mystery behind it requires astronomers to figure out the history of the three objects. A white dwarf is what remains when a star of similar mass to our sun reaches the end of its life. As it begins to run low on hydrogen for nuclear fusion reactions in its core, the star's energy generation begins to falter and its interior starts to contract under gravity . This has the effect of raising the temperature throughout the star, causing an outer shell of hydrogen to ignite its own fusion reactions. The star promptly bloats to become a red giant tens or even hundreds of millions of kilometers across.
