A Falcon 9 rocket lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base tonight at 11:20 p.m. EST (8:20 p.m. local California time; 0420 GMT on Nov. 21), on a Space Force mission called USSF-317. The Falcon 9's first stage came back to Earth about 8.5 minutes later, touching down in the Pacific Ocean atop the SpaceX drone ship "Of Course I Still Love You." It was the 11th launch and landing for this booster, which is designated B1100. (Image credit: SpaceX) SpaceX ended its launch webcast just after that touchdown, so we didn't get to see the Falcon 9's upper stage power its way to orbit. USSF-317 is a classified mission, so we don't know what the upper stage is carrying or where exactly it's headed. However, some experts think that USSF-317 may be hauling a batch of SpaceX's Starshield satellites to low Earth orbit (LEO) for the Space Force's Space Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio, which helps the military keep tabs on potential targets around the world.
