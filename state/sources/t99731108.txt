NASA's DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test) mission made history on Sept. 26, 2022, when it slammed into Dimorphos , the small moonlet of the much larger asteroid Didymos . The mission tested if such an impact could be deployed to divert an asteroid on a collision course with Earth. Scientists have known for some time that this deflection attempt was successful; the system's orbit around the sun was lengthened by 33 minutes, but the European Space Agency's (ESA) Hera mission launched in October 2024 to travel the 120 million miles (194 million kilometers) to the Didymos system to observe the effect of the DART impact in greater detail. Now, the spacecraft has revealed that DART flattened one face of Dimorphos with a stadium-sized crater and set it tumbling chaotically. The impact also appears to have blasted out hundreds of previously unknown boulders that are now circling the binary asteroids. "Scientists debated whether Dimorphos had sustained an impact crater, changed its shape, or stayed the same," Hera principal investigator Patrick Michel, Director of Research at the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur, said in a statement. "Much to our surprise, the answer turns out to be all three at once! The face struck by DART has been flattened into an impact crater the size of a sports stadium, which extends across the asteroid's entire width."
