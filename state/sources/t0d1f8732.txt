Photograph: Paul Kennedy/Alamy Terrawatch Oceans How a new way of measuring tides could make flood forecasts more accurate Analysis of differences in tidal height across beaches could lead to more localised tide and flood forecasts Kate Ravilious Wed 25 Nov 2026 01.00 EST Share Prefer the Guardian on Google T he sea might look level, but it turns out tidal height can vary by as much as a metre across the length of a single beach, new research shows. This development is important when it comes to flood predictions and when forecasting which locations are most vulnerable to storm surges. Up until now, our knowledge of tidal levels has been limited. Tide gauges tell us about a specific location, and satellite radar measurements typically average across tens of kilometres. But instead of using tide gauges, researchers at Technische Universität München and the University of Oxford analysed satellite photos of shorelines, using the beach itself as a giant ruler to measure tidal height for every 100 metres of beach. Assessing 40 years of satellite photographs from Pacific Ocean shorelines, they found wide variations in tidal height, with New Zealand’s South Taranaki Bight recording a difference in tidal height of almost a metre across the 56-mile (90km) bay. The research, which was published in Nature Communications: Earth and Environment , showed how tides varied significantly over relatively short distances.
