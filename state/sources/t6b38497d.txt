All 17 of the Malta-registered Med Emre's crew members were rescued but the chief engineer later died in hospital. Six people were treated for minor injuries. There had been concerns of a major oil spillage in the western Mediterranean but an inspection showed that the ship's fuel tanks are intact. An investigation is under way into what happened. The vessel had been travelling from Algeciras near Gibraltar to Barcelona and is expected to arrive in Cartagena on Monday in what is being seen as a challenging rescue operation. In a statement on Friday, the Spanish transport ministry said the tanker would be towed to Cartagena "as it is the only one [port] that meets the technical requirements to carry out cargo extraction and repair work". Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez told Cadena SER radio network he hoped the tanker could be taken to port "in the coming hours, in the coming days". "The information suggests that, indeed... this leak is not happening", he added. The Spanish authorities provided no details about how the diesel would be extracted. Meanwhile, pictures have emerged showing emergency workers inspecting the tanker's hull. Any possible environmental damage is being assessed by air and pollution control vessel Clara Campoamor, which is owned by Spain's national maritime rescue service. Elena Delgado, maritime captain of the port of Valencia, earlier told news agency AFP that a small slick which had formed around the vessel had "disappeared". "The situation is stabilised," she said, adding that further inspections would be carried out. Regional emergencies director Pablo Gárriz said the tanker had not completely sunk as originally reported. The vessel remains partially submerged. The Med Emre capsized 25 nautical miles (46 km; 29 miles) from Ibiza's coastline and 57 nautical miles from Valencia. Rescuers in Valencia received a distress call late on Wednesday, according to Spanish media. Minutes later, the vessel reported a leak and the crew said it was leaning at a 90-degree angle. It capsized in the early hours on Thursday. Ibiza's marine environment was added to the Unesco World Heritage List in 1999. In particular, it is home to dense prairies of Posidonia oceanic seagrass, which is found only in the Mediterranean basin and plays a vital role in supporting local marine biodiversity. In 2002, Spain experienced one of its worst environmental disasters when the Prestige oil tanker ran into trouble off Galicia's coastline, in the country's north-west. More than 60,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil spilled into the sea.
