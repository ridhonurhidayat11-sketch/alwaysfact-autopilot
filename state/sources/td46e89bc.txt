The Perseverance rover and Mars Odyssey orbiter have stood down from science work due to technical problems, NASA announced on Thursday (Nov. 19). Perseverance has been sidelined by an antenna issue, and the venerable Odyssey is dealing with a balky reaction wheel. NASA hopes both can get back to work at Mars by the end of November. Communication issues for Perseverance Perseverance landed inside Mars' Jezero Crater in February 2021, tasked with assessing the area's potential to support past life and with collecting dozens of samples for future return to Earth. It has done all this and more. The Perseverance team has determined that Jezero hosted a potentially habitable lake billions of years ago. And the car-sized rover has found a wealth of complex organic compounds in Martian rock, some of which might themselves be signs of life (though assessing this hypothesis fully will likely require bringing Perseverance's samples to Earth for analysis, scientists stress).
