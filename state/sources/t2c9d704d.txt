This researcher has clues Cornell computational social scientist Yian Yin turns the tools of science on science itself. He’s studying how AI-assisted submissions are changing what gets through peer review. Courtesy of Y. Yin By Anna Mattson 13 hours ago Share this: Share Share via email (Opens in new window) Email Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook Share on Reddit (Opens in new window) Reddit Share on X (Opens in new window) X Print (Opens in new window) Print Yian Yin seeks to understand the dynamics of science itself. The computational social scientist is particularly interested in how innovations happen and how they may be accelerated. “I want to understand what the secret sources or recipes of success are,” says Yin, of Cornell University. He applied machine learning techniques to accepted and rejected research proposals to understand how scientists learn from failures. What he found was that scientists fail, and they fail often. Some switched gears after some rejections, but others who continued their research and incorporated feedback found success at a faster rate. Those who changed ideas or scrapped original proposals did not leverage a lot from their experience. Journals are receiving more AI-assisted submissions than ever before, which is leading the peer-review process to falter. More seemingly well-written papers that lack substance are getting through, he found. Coming up with large-scale solutions requires looking at the issue in a measurable way, Yin says. “Considering both the technological and social side of scientific ecosystems would be something that’s really beneficial for the ongoing public debate and discussion about how we accelerate science in the age of artificial intelligence,” Yin says.
