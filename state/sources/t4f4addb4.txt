Here's what you need to know. As an inner planet, Venus never strays far from the horizon, at least compared to outer planets like Jupiter and Mars which can be visible all night long during opposition, when they shine opposite the sun in Earth's sky. Instead, Venus alternates between morning and evening appearances. All the while, its shifting moon-like phases and ever-changing proximity to Earth causes its brightness to wax and wane. When is Venus at its most dazzling? Venus will reach peak brightness during its current morning apparition at 1:37 p.m. EST (1837 GMT) on Nov. 27 according to the astronomy website In-The-Sky . Stargazers in the U.S. could get an incredible view of Venus in the predawn hours of the coming week, as the planet climbs into the southeastern sky ahead of the rising sun, with Mercury clinging close to the horizon below. For a closer look at craters and the terminator, make sure to grab a 9mm or 12mm eyepiece and get stronger magnification. _ Celestron Nexstar 4SE review The bright star Spica will glow less than five degrees away from Venus before dawn on Nov. 27 — roughly the width of your three middle fingers held at arm's length against the night sky.
