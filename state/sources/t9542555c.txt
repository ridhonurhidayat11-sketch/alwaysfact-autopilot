You know them well, they're always there, and they never seem to change. A case in point is Cassiopeia, the queen. Its beautiful name comes from Greek mythology — the mother of Andromeda — and its shape is said to represent a queen sitting on a throne. To a stargazer, Cassiopeia is best known as a prominent northern constellation shaped like the letter W. It's easy to find — look north, find five reasonably bright stars making a W, congratulate yourself on finding Cassiopeia and use it to get somewhere more interesting. It's so often a signpost, not a destination. But look north a few hours after dark this month, and you'll notice the W has gone. The five stars are still there, but the shape you may have memorized looks like it's been picked up and turned over. It looks more like an E or an M, depending on when you look. Now try and look for the Big Dipper /the Plough. It's gone. Nothing mysterious has happened, of course. Cassiopeia is doing exactly what it always does, but it's a reminder that the northern sky is where you can easily watch the stars move — and the seasons turn.
