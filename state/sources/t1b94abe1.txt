The Mars rover Curiosity was the biggest and most powerful rover anyone had ever sent to the Red Planet. The 2-ton science laboratory is about the size of a small SUV. The mission's goal was to search for habitable environments on Mars , or evidence that the planet could have been habitable in the past. It launched on an Atlas V rocket from Cape Canaveral Air Force Station in Florida. About nine months later, Curiosity made a challenging landing on Mars. It successfully used a new landing technology called a sky crane to gently lower it to the surface. NASA's Curiosity rover lifts off atop a United Launch Alliance Atlas V rocket on Nov. 26, 2011 from Florida's Cape Canaveral Air Force Station. (Image credit: collectSPACE/Robert Z. Pearlman ) Why it mattered It may be hard to imagine now, but 15 years ago, the idea of a nuclear-powered rover on Mars was just a concept. When the Curiosity rover launched in 2011, it made that idea a reality the car-size probe (using a plutonium-based radioisotope thermoelectric generator that converts heat from radioactive decay into power) as it headed to the Red Planet. Curiosity was the largest rover ever launched to Mars, following up on the success of the tiny Sojourner rover in 1997 and the larger twin rovers Spirit and Opportunity in 2004. Its sky crane landing system was novel at the time, and fully automated. Once it began its descent in August 2012, NASA could only wait through "seven minutes of terror" to see how it turned out.
