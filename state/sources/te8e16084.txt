The islands, which are being planted by community groups, are designed to encourage wildlife and help clean the water. The project will create almost 250 square metres of new wildlife habitat which is expected to last about 20 years. Glasgow 2026 organisers say they are funding the projects as part of their commitment to greener games. Image source, Colin Mearns/Glasgow 2026 Image caption, Ellen Hebdon says the plants' roots play an important role in preventing and removing harmful algae from the water The islands are manufactured by a Moray-based company which has been exporting them around the world. Biomatrix Water Solutions in Forres has installed islands in Boston, Venice and London. They have also been floated onto the River Clyde at the Glasgow Science Centre as part of an unrelated project. The new islands are being installed at Victoria Park, Elder Park, Richmond Park, Queen's Park and Hogganfield Loch. Ellen Hebdon, the company's operations manager, said the plants' roots played an important role in preventing and removing harmful algae from the water. "It's not an overnight solution, but over a period of time, you will see benefits in the clarity and the cleanliness of the water," she said. Image source, Biomatrix Water Image caption, Floating islands have been installed at the Glasgow Science Centre The islands are made up of recycled plastic materials, including drinking water pipes, to create buoyancy and are held together using marine-grade stainless steel. They are then topped with a lightweight clay aggregate, called leca, into which the plants are inserted. The aquatic plants include a mix of evergreen and flowering native species such as marsh marigolds, water forget-me-nots and ragged robin. Their roots underneath the islands provide shelter for aquatic wildlife. The location, in the middle of the water, also provides nesting spaces for birds, which would be largely free from disturbance. Local schools and "friends of the park" groups are helping with the planting and maintenance. Games organisers say sustainability is an important delivery factor with all competition venues powered by renewable electricity and electric or hybrid vehicles making up two thirds of the transport fleet. Glasgow 2026's head of sustainability, Selina Donald, said the city council had been looking at creating floating islands but did not have the funding. "I really saw the pride in all the parks, the pride in the green spaces, the pride in the natural environment across the city," she said. "And so really, for us, it was a way that we can celebrate and support that."
