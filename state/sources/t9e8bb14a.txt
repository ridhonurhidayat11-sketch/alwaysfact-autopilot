Jump To: Simple stargazing Slide selection Limited library I didn’t expect much as I unboxed the Brainstorm Night Sky; after all, I’m significantly older than this educational star projector’s target audience. Perhaps I’d come away with a sense of wonder and delight, coupled with mild irritation at having to hunt down three AA batteries to power it. Instead, I was filled with envy as the Brainstorm Night Sky reminded me just what I was missing, thanks to the rigors of light pollution . A few minutes after powering it down, I was already making plans for my first dark sky trip, yearning for a view that rivaled the Brainstorm’s cosmic images. Brainstorm Night Sky Projector review Whatever your age, the Brainstorm Night Sky is so simple to use The Brainstorm Night Sky projector comes with four or six slides, depending on whether you buy the blue or gray version (Image credit: Chris McMullen) Draw the curtains, press the Night Sky’s single button (tilting the projector and twisting the focus as appropriate) and it projects a circular image onto a ceiling or wall. Press the button again and the projection slowly begins to rotate, slowly but stutter-free. It really is that simple to operate and, aimed at budding astronomers aged eight or above, it's reasonably lightweight without feeling flimsy.
