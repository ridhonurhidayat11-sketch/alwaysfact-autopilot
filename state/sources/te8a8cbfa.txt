The answer lies in the identity and maturity of the affected cells. View author publications Search author on: PubMed Google Scholar Email Bluesky Facebook LinkedIn Reddit Whatsapp X Save article View saved research Mutations in the cancer-associated gene PIK3CA can cause vascular malformations 1 . These abnormalities can affect people from birth or can be acquired over a person’s lifetime, and they involve veins, capillaries and lymphatic vessels — but rarely arteries 2 , 3 . Why are arteries spared? This mystery is investigated by Sabata et al. 4 in their paper in Nature .
