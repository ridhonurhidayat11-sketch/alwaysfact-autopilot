They are calling for the Environment Agency to stop the sewage treatment plant in Peterchurch, Herefordshire, discharging into the River Dore during periods of no flow. At the moment, Welsh Water legally discharges directly into the river, but campaigners said its permit assumed the effluent would be diluted by the river flow. The company said the plant was operating in accordance with the permit, but it was liaising with residents over their concerns. Locals have formed a Citizen's Science Project for the Dore catchment and say lab tests have shown the waste contains "high levels of nitrate and phosphorus" as well as harmful bacteria including "worrying amounts of E. coli". Image source, Alice Elliott Image caption, The River Dore that feeds into the Monnow and then the Wye, has seen long periods of 'no flow' over the last 15 years Alice Elliott from the group said they had seen periods of drought affecting the river level for several years: "Where the river stops flowing it goes completely dry, absolutely no flow. "The sewage treatment works is continually discharging onto a dry riverbed and it's created a large pool of treated but undiluted effluent." The group said it had samples from the site tested in a laboratory and were seeing "high levels of nitrate and phosphorus". "Treated effluent isn't clean sanitised water." said Elliott. "We've noticed that it contains worrying amounts of E. coli and other bacteria that are harmful to health... Normally it would get diluted down and that's what the permit allows". It is thought the area is surrounded by a permeable gravel bed and residents are concerned the "extensive pool" is being allowed to "permeate through the ground and through the soil around it." The dry bed also becomes a lure for children playing during drought conditions. "It was like a sunken road, it was an exciting environment and the children were skipping along, unaware that a few metres away is a big pool of treated sewage." Image source, Alice Elliott Image caption, Rosie Lane collects samples from the "extensive pool" of effluent sitting on the dry river bed near the sewage treatment plant in Peterchurch The group has demanded answers from both Welsh Water and the Environment Agency. "What we've asked is how do they go about assessing and issuing their permits for Welsh Water? What are they doing in terms of assessing the environmental impact when they know there is a period of no flow. What action... what plans are they putting in place to combat that?" said Elliott. The River Dore flows into the Monnow, which then joins the River Wye at Monmouth - a river that has had it's fair share of publicity over alleged contamination, leading to it becoming the first river in the UK to get its own special protection charter. Image source, Alice Elliott Image caption, The outlet from the Welsh Water sewage treatment plant as it is released into the River Dore at Peterchurch An Environment Agency spokesperson said: "Protecting and improving water quality is a priority for the Environment Agency, and we understand the concerns raised by local residents about conditions in the River Dore during prolonged dry weather. "Peterchurch sewage treatment works' discharge is permitted and currently understood to be compliant. We continue to monitor conditions in the River Dore." A spokesperson for Welsh Water said: "Peterchurch waste water treatment works is operating as it should and within the permit conditions issued by our regulator. "The treated effluent is routinely sampled and tested and we are liaising with the Dore Citizen Science group and the Environment Agency on this matter."
