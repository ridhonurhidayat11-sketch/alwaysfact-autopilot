Credits: Image: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, Tea Temim (Columbia), Rick Arendt (UMBC), Dan Milisavljevic (Purdue University); Image Processing: Joseph DePasquale (STScI) NASA Webb Mission Team Goddard Space Flight Center Nov 23, 2026 Article Contents Image: Dust In Cassiopeia A (MIRI Image) Image: Dust In Cassiopeia A, North Up (MIRI) Image: Dust in Cassiopeia A (Spitzer and Webb) Downloads & Related Information Related Links NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope has painted a vibrant tapestry of the legendary supernova remnant Cassiopeia A (Cas A). Remnants like these are the clouds of gas and dust that remain following the explosion of a massive star and will eventually seed a new generation of stars and planets. This high-resolution, mid-infrared observation from Webb reveals new details that are helping astronomers chronicle the structure and evolution of Cas A’s original star. The Webb data supports a scenario in which the interior of the star underwent a dramatic change, possibly just hours before its demise. Image: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, Tea Temim (Columbia University), Rick Arendt (UMBC), Dan Milisavljevic (Purdue University); Image Processing: Joseph DePasquale (STScI) Stars begin their lives as large clouds of mostly hydrogen gas and cosmic dust called molecular clouds. Eventually, gravity causes these molecular clouds to collapse, the material heats up, and protostars — infant stars — form. Under the constant pressure of gravity, hydrogen within a protostar begins to fuse into helium, which marks the point where protostars evolve into full-fledged stars like our Sun.
