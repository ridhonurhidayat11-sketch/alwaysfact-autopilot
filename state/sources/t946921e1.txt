I've seen nine total solar eclipses across the world, but this one promises to be different. For a remarkable six minutes and 22 seconds, the moon will completely cover the sun, plunging Egypt's ancient temples and monuments into eerie twilight as the solar corona bursts into view. I've been calling it the "eclipse of the century" for many years, and for good reason. It will be the longest total solar eclipse until 2114 — and the longest on land of the 21st century — and it will take place in one of the most extraordinary places on Earth. What could go wrong? I've chased eclipses long enough to know that the celestial mechanics are the easy part. The moon will arrive exactly on schedule — that's guaranteed. Everything else — the weather, the crowds, the transport, the equipment and the people — is rather less predictable. From the possibility of dust storms to uncertainty about whether boats will even be allowed to move on the Nile, there are plenty of reasons to be slightly wary about what awaits eclipse chasers after the ultimate bragging rights on Aug. 2, 2027.
