The Leamington High Line Community Interest Company (CIC) has raised enough money to fix a ladder and platform to the first stretch of the viaduct in Bath Place, giving them access to the former track bed. It comes as the first set of conceptual drawings of what the proposed gardens would look like are published. Derrick Knight, a director of the CIC said: "We're going to turn this neglected heritage structure into a place the town can enjoy for generations." The aim is to create five themed gardens connecting the railway station with Leamington's creative quarter and the eastern part of the town. It is estimated that the scheme, which is being funded through grants and donations, will cost between £10m and £50m and could take up to 10 years to complete. Image source, Greenwood-Hill/Leamington High Line CIC Image caption, Five themed gardens running the length of the viaduct are planned, with an estimated cost of up to £50m The viaduct was built in 1844 and runs from Lower Avenue to the Victoria Business Centre on Neilston Street, a distance of around 500 metres. It originally carried both goods and passengers from the late 1800s up to the 1960s. It then closed, but the brick-and-masonry arches were not demolished. Now, 60 years on, campaigners want to turn it into a green travel route over the town. Alan Bartlett, another director of the project, said: "The first job is to survey carefully for bat roosts and take off some of the self-seeded plants and trees, which are breaking up the brickwork and pushing roots into the arches below, where local businesses trade. "So clearing them is the first good thing we can do for the structure and for our neighbours." Image source, Google/Leamington High Line CIC Image caption, The aim is to create five gardens running on the viaduct from Lower Avenue through to the Victoria Business Centre on Neilston Street in Leamington Spa If approved, the plan would see gardens with specific themes created on top of the old track bed. They would be: Faith Garden, including a renovated railway carriage as a cafe and bar History Garden, telling the story of the viaduct's construction in 1844 Hope Garden, a community allotment with a long table running the length of it Solar Garden, with pergolas featuring energy-generating solar panels Sky Garden, the longest of the gardens with seating and a space for community events Ian Dunning, another of the CIC's directors, said while the first steps in the project have been taken, he estimated most of it could be open to the public in five years time. "The funding in terms of grants still needs to be unlocked and we're in the process of submitting funding applications." He thinks the whole scheme would cost between £10m and £50m, although he is confident that the final bill would be lower than than the higher end estimate. The CIC has appointed the London-based landscape architects Greenwood-Hill to create the initial vision of the planting and scheme. A spokesperson for the design firm said "the sketches set out some early ideas for how the High Line could become a greener, more accessible and welcoming place for people and wildlife alike. "It's an exciting community-led vision, and we hope these initial concepts help "people imagine the potential of this unique asset in the heart of Leamington." From West Village to Warwickshire Similar projects have been constructed in cities around the world, probably most famously in Manhattan, New York, , external where a stretch of elevated rail line was saved from demolition in the 80s with the first area of parkland opening in 2009. Seoul in South Korea has a similar park , external , constructed on a stretch of disused highway over the city. Supporters say they can help promote walking and cycling and sustainably re-use obsolete infrastructure. However, lack of funding can be an obstacle, as demonstrated in in Camden, north London , external , where work on a high line project has stalled because of rising costs.
