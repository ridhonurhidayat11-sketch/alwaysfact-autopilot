By Rachel Foley BBC Weather Published 24 November 2026, 11:38 GMT Updated 11:40 GMT Stormy weather on both US coasts could disrupt travel for millions over the Thanksgiving period. The American Automobile Association (AAA) predicts 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home for Thanksgiving between Tuesday 24 November and Monday 30 November. The US National Weather Service says a series of Pacific storm systems will bring widespread rain, mountain snow, gusty winds and high surf to the West Coast this week, with coastal flood watches widely in place. Meanwhile, Wednesday will see a storm off the south-east coast intensify and run north along the eastern seaboard. This will develop into a powerful Nor'easter storm over Thanksgiving, bringing floods, strong winds and heavy rain. West Coast flood risk The West Coast will be badly affected in the run up to Thanksgiving, with a strong Pacific storm expected to move into the north-west on Tuesday night and affect the area throughout Wednesday. The storm will bring particularly heavy rain because it will tap into an atmospheric river , a huge, invisible ribbon of water vapour. Impacts will include strong winds, heavy rain at lower elevations and snow in the mountains from western Washington to Northern California. The AAA predicts the vast majority of holiday travellers, 72.9 million, will travel by car. Drivers could see significant delays along I-5 between Seattle and Sacramento as a result of the storm. Mountain travel could also be affected on the I-80 and I-90. The heaviest rain is expected to miss Los Angeles and much of Southern California. Image caption, Moderate to major coastal flooding is forecast for the California coast With the full Beaver moon on Tuesday , the West Coast is also expecting the highest tides of the year, coupled with the incoming storm. A coastal flood advisory is in place for the Pacific Northwest and parts of California. The worst rain is expected to come late Wednesday into Thursday, with another storm forecast for Friday evening. The San Francisco mayor has warned that the city is bracing for significant weather impacts in the coming days. End of X post by Daniel Lurie 丹尼爾·羅偉 Developing Nor'easter Image caption, A developing storm will impact the Northeast of the US on Thanksgiving Day On the East Coast, a developing Nor'easter storm is expected to bring heavy rain, strong winds, and coastal flooding to the south-east. It will hit Florida and the Carolinas on Wednesday, before moving northwards into the mid-Atlantic and Northeast on Thanksgiving Day. The National Weather Service is predicting wind gusts of more than 40mph (64km/h) inland along the I-95 corridor, which connects 15 states and Washington DC. Heavy rain is expected in New York City on Thanksgiving Day, with the city's emergency management authorities warning of "fowl weather" and a chance of coastal flooding. The forecast has also raised questions about whether large balloons will be allowed to fly during the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade. Under city regulations , external , full-size balloons are not allowed if sustained wind speeds are higher than 23mph (37km/h) or gusts surpass 35mph (56km/h). End of X post 2 by NYC Emergency Management Image caption, Much of the US east coast will experience heavy rain over the holiday Flight disruption A major worry for the 5.96 million travellers who will take domestic flights for Thanksgiving is whether the weather will cause flight disruption. Flights across the US have recently returned to normal after the national air-traffic control system recovered from a technical failure that prompted a rare national ground stop . San Francisco and Oakland airports are under a coastal flood warning , external , with United Airlines allowing some passengers flying through San Francisco International Airport (SFO) on Wednesday to change their plans for free , external . Both airports sit along the shoreline, which could be impacted by the exceptionally high king tides expected in the coming days. These tides are being strengthened by the strongest El Niño on record . However, forecasters have lowered their estimate for Wednesday's high tide from about 3 feet above a normal high tide to 2.7 feet, which could help runways stay dry. SFO airport authorities are still warning of potential weather impacts , external over the Thanksgiving period.
