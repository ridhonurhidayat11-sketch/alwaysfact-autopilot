The robotic Dragon capsule will undock from the International Space Station (ISS) today at around 12:05 p.m. EST (1705 GMT), if all goes according to plan. You can watch Dragon's departure live here at Space.com, courtesy of NASA. Coverage will begin at 11:45 a.m. EST (1645 GMT). A SpaceX Dragon spacecraft supporting the company's 34th commercial resupply services mission for NASA departs the International Space Station on June 16, 2026. (Image credit: NASA/Jessica Meir) If Dragon does indeed leave the ISS today, it will splash down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of California on Saturday (Nov. 21) at about 4:55 p.m. EST (2155 GMT). There will be no livestream for its reentry and splashdown. A safe return to Earth requires relatively calm seas, so NASA and SpaceX are keeping a close eye on the weather in the splashdown zone. If conditions aren't great for a Saturday afternoon return, Dragon will stay docked to the ISS for a bit longer, until things improve. The capsule arrived at the ISS on Oct. 15 , delivering more than 6,300 pounds (2,860 kilograms) of food, scientific gear and other supplies to the astronauts aboard the orbiting lab. It will bring a lot of stuff down to Earth, too.
