Image source, Darren Staples via Getty Images By Ben Rich Lead Weather Presenter Published 2 hours ago Christmas Day is a month away and as the festive season approaches you may start to wonder - could 2026 be a 'white Christmas'? The likelihood is actually higher than you might imagine. Over the past 65 years, there have been 41 white Christmases, meaning we are more likely than not to see one somewhere in the UK. Forecasting the chances of a month ahead of the big day is a bit like staring into a crystal ball - but are there any early omens of snow on the festive horizon? How do we define a white Christmas? For a Christmas to be officially declared as 'white', a single snowflake needs to be recorded falling at any point during 25 December, at any of the Met Office's network of around 300 weather stations. It means there doesn't have to be a thick covering of snow on the ground to qualify. Indeed, the landscape could be beautifully festive and white with lying snow, but if no new snow falls on Christmas Day it cannot technically be called a white Christmas. The last widespread white Christmas was in 2010, when snow fell at 19% of measuring stations and a significant 83% of stations reported snow lying on the ground. Every year from 2020 to 2023 was officially a white Christmas, however very few places actually reported settling snow. Image caption, In December 2010 the UK experienced low temperatures and significant snowfalls. Lying snow was reported in many places on Christmas Day Are there signs we could have a white Christmas in the UK this year? A number of factors will influence the UK's weather in the run-up to the festive season. A major one is the record-breaking El Niño which is, scientists predict, approaching its peak in the Pacific. El Niño's strongest impacts tend to be felt close to where the phenomenon occurs, in the tropical Pacific, but the energy it releases into the atmosphere can send ripples through weather patterns across the globe. In the UK, the impacts tend to be subtle and unreliable. Strong El Niño events can increase the odds of wet and stormy weather during the late autumn and early winter, and some studies suggest they can raise the chances of colder weather later in the season - but the evidence from past El Niños is decidedly mixed. And this is far from the only factor driving our weather. Will El Niño and the polar vortex bring snow to the UK this winter? Published 9 October Stormy autumn, cold winter - how might El Niño affect the UK weather? Published 3 September Earlier in November the winds of the stratospheric polar vortex weakened and reversed, marking the earliest sudden stratospheric warming event on record. This phenomenon tends to occur much later in winter and can - but doesn't always - weaken the jet stream, increasing the chance of cold northerly or easterly winds. Occurring so early in the season, the effects this time are harder to quantify. It is not known what - if any - impact it will have on the weather over the weeks ahead. The latest predictions from scientists at the Met Office , external , taking these and other factors into account, suggest there could be an increased likelihood of colder and drier-than-average conditions during the winter months of December, January and February. However this only represents an increased probability of these conditions and - even if this occurs across the season as a whole - there are still very likely to be shorter-term periods of milder, wetter weather. When will we know for sure if Christmas will be white? It is often tricky to accurately forecast snow in the UK, even a day or two in advance. Snow requires just the right combination of cold air and moisture, and small changes in either of these elements can mean predicted snow actually falls as rain or vice versa. It goes without saying that longer range snow forecasts are even more challenging and with Christmas still a month away it is impossible to offer precise predictions.
