(stock image) (Image credit: rusm/Getty Images) As millions of Americans hit the roads, rails and skies to celebrate Turkey Day, the weather will likely bring major holiday headaches to both sides of the country. A powerful coastal storm will lash the eastern half of the nation as a Pacific system brings rain and mountain snow to the West. Here’s your Thanksgiving week forecast from NOAA’s Weather Prediction Center : Wednesday: Storms on the horizon A developing autumn storm will bring scattered showers and a few thunderstorms across the lower Mississippi Valley and Southeast for the busy pre-Thanksgiving travel day. Later Wednesday into Thursday morning, chillier air will wrap into the mid-Mississippi Valley, bringing the chance for some significant wet snow around the St. Louis metro area and the surrounding region. In the West, a Pacific system will approach the coast and bring the chance for showers to the Pacific Northwest and Northern California. Coastal flooding will also be likely along the West Coast during high tides. Thursday, Thanksgiving Day: Very windy and rainy for some A potent Thanksgiving Day storm will bring the threat for heavy rain, high winds and flooding spanning the Ohio Valley to the Eastern Seaboard. Strong winds, heavy downpours and coastal flooding will likely impact Thursday morning travel, Turkey Trots and Thanksgiving Day parades from the Carolinas to southern New England. Across the mid-Mississippi Valley, a wintry mix of chilly rain and accumulating snow will continue through the first half of the day, bringing a White Thanksgiving and travel troubles to the region. Further west, the Pacific Northwest will see another chance for widespread rain and mountain snow, with dry, seasonal weather expected in the Southwestern U.S. Before heading out on Thanksgiving Day, be sure to check your local forecast . Friday: Coastal storms continue to bring impacts Heavy rain will linger into Friday for much of New England, while scattered showers and a little rain/snow mix are expected to fall across parts of the Great Lakes and Ohio Valley. Across the Pacific Northwest, travelers heading home ahead of the weekend might have to contend with more rounds of showers and mountain snow. Along the southern tier of the U.S., mostly dry and seasonal weather is on tap for shoppers looking for holiday sales. Saturday: Eastern storm exits as another one eyes the West The strong coastal storm looks to finally push away from the Northeast on Saturday. Before it does, scattered showers or a rain-snow mix will be likely from the Great Lakes to New England during the day. Farther west, some rain and higher elevation snow are expected as a system moves across the central and northern Rockies. Dry conditions are expected across much of the southern tier of the U.S. Sunday: Some travel trouble spots remain Many traveling home from the long weekend on Sunday, might require a lot of patience. Yet another Pacific storm will bring chances for rain and mountain snow, from California to Washington state. Wet roads may make a return across the Southeast and Florida, as well as a chance for some rain and snow mixed across the Great Lakes.
