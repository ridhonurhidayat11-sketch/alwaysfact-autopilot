The bench in Newent, Gloucestershire, celebrates Eric Freeman, who promoted Gloucester cattle, Cotswold sheep and Gloucestershire Old Spot pigs on his farm in Taynton, and is credited with helping to revive wassailing in the county. He died in 2023 aged 91, and his friend, cheese maker Charles Martell, remembered: "It would take Eric two hours to get from the library to the post office because he knew everybody and he didn't do short chats." Freeman's granddaughter, Natasha James, said he would have been "thrilled" by the honour. In 1972, when Gloucester cattle were near extinction, he and some friends went to an auction where the last herd was up for sale, and decided to save them. "Lose the breed, and lose all the things they mean to Gloucestershire," he said. In 2013, King Charles III presented him with a lifetime achievement award for his services to conservation. He was also a regular on BBC Radio Gloucestershire , with his Freeman of the Farm feature, and a quote from one of his broadcasts features on the plaque: "The town is full of character, it's just my home, and it's a lovely old place. I've got my heart in it." Around 100 people came for the unveiling of the bench, which was a gift from the Cotswold Cart Horse Society, which Freeman helped to establish in 1996. Image caption, Paul Barnett from the Cart Horse Society said Freeman was a "great man" whose legacy continues "He was a character and knew about the old ways of farming," his granddaughter said. "He was a fountain of knowledge on the local area; I hope it [the bench] will give people a chance to understand who he was and the place where he lived."
