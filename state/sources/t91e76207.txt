Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link Peruvian authorities have updated the number of tarantulas seized in a recent wildlife trafficking case from 3,000 to more than 12,000, according to South American media reports . The final count of 12,796 seized spiders was reportedly announced by the Peruvian National Police (PNP). The authorities said they believe these arachnids are worth more than half a million euros (around $548,000) on the international market. On Nov. 4, 2026, the Peruvian National Forest and Wildlife Service (SERFOR) announced it seized thousands of tarantulas at Jorge Chávez International Airport in Lima, hidden inside socks and plastic containers in luggage destined for Germany. Authorities arrested two German citizens and a Polish national, including a “renowned tarantula researcher” who was not publicly identified, and detained them for further investigation at the time. The seized tarantulas included individuals in different life stages, from eggs and hatchlings to adults, and came from across Peru. They included blue bird eaters ( Thrixopelma lagunas ), Bolivian blue leg bird eater ( Pamphobeteus antinous ), members of the genus Pamphobeteus and several newly described species. Authorities said the alleged traffickers collected the spiders from the wild to sell as exotic pets to collectors and for research. None of the seized species are internationally protected under CITES, the global wildlife treaty. However, they are protected nationally under Peruvian laws, and removing them from the wild is an offense. In a TV program , General José Manuel Cruz Chamba from the PNP reportedly identified one of the arrested individuals as Auer Hans Werner, a German national. Werner was previously arrested in 2021 in Colombia, along with another German, a Polish and a Venezuelan citizen, who were accused of trafficking nine tarantulas and an egg, collected from the wild. Werner is listed as a trader at the upcoming Terraristika Hamm — which is promoted as the world’s largest trade fair for terrarium animals, including tarantulas and other exotic, protected reptiles and arachnids — taking place in December in Germany. In 2025, Galápagos lava lizard ( Microlophus albemarlensis ) and the Santa Cruz lava lizard ( Microlophus indefatigabilis ), both endemic and protected on the archipelago, were offered for sale at the Hamm fair, according to Pro Wildlife, a German NGO, which documents cases of “stolen wildlife” to Europe violating national regulations. On the program, Cruz Chamba said he believes an international trafficking network is involved in the latest Peru case, given the high number of seized tarantulas. He indicated the arrested individuals may face 11-20 years in prison if convicted. “The fact that these individuals just carry on shows that the penalties aren’t severe enough to act as a deterrent. As long as they only face small fines, smuggling is worth it,” Sandra Altherr of Pro Wildlife told Mongabay. “In this case, there is hope that a longer prison sentence might be imposed.” Authorities continue to investigate the case.
