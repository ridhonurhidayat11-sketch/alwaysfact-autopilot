The mission will be the third flight of the company’s Cygnus XL, the larger, more cargo-capable version of the company’s solar-powered spacecraft. The Cygnus XL will launch aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from the Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. Following arrival to the station, astronauts aboard the space station will use the Canadarm2 to grapple the spacecraft before ground controllers robotically install it to the Unity module’s Earth-facing port for cargo unloading. Lovell Jr. Lovell flew four missions, including Gemini VII, Gemini XII, Apollo 8, and the Apollo 13 mission around the Moon. Credit: NASA NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney will be on duty during the Cygnus spacecraft’s approach and rendezvous. Watkins will be at the controls of the Canadarm2 robotic arm, ready to capture Cygnus as Delaney monitors the spacecraft’s arrival. Credit: NASA Science highlights In addition to food, supplies, and equipment for the crew, Cygnus will deliver several new experiments aimed at improving drug delivery for cancer and osteoarthritis treatment, predicting cancer spread, advancing treatments for muscle loss, improving radiation sensing, and supporting food production for future long-duration missions. This research includes: Cardinal Muscle 2.0 Preflight image of the Biocell bioreactor system used to maintain engineered muscle tissue samples as part of the Advancing Therapeutic Treatment of Sarcopenia (Cardinal Muscle 2.0) investigation. Aboard the International Space Station, this investigation tests the effects of potential therapeutics on human muscle cell cultures. The research may help accelerate development of treatments for age-related muscle loss on Earth and support astronaut health during long-duration missions. Credit: Palo Alto Veteran’s Institute for Research Cardinal Muscle 2.0 uses the microgravity environment to explore potential treatments for muscle loss. Muscle atrophy and strength loss experienced by astronauts after spaceflight share many features with age-related muscle decline on Earth and occur more rapidly in microgravity without exercise.
