Water levels across the US state's vast coastline could reach over 8ft (2.4 metres) in some areas on Wednesday and Thursday, according to the National Weather Service (NWS) - breaking records set just last month. The NWS has warned parts of California could face major coastal flooding, which is likely to be significant in low-lying areas. There have already been some reports of flooding. Meanwhile, storms are also forecast for the Pacific Northwest, including the Canadian province of British Columbia, and eastern parts of the US. Portions of a pier in northern California came crashing down into the ocean on Wednesday as high tides and strong waves battered the structure, video showed. Beachgoers have been advised to stay away from the water's edge as large waves could suddenly form. Such waves could be damaging to piers and jetties, and could capsize small vessels, the NWS warned. Flooding has already been reported in the San Francisco Bay Area. In San Diego, a high tide of 8 ft 11in was recorded on Wednesday morning local time - potentially breaking the record for highest tide measured in the area, which was just set late last month at 7.98ft. In Los Angeles, the tide on Wednesday was thought to be as high 8.19 - the record for the city that was set only weeks ago 27 October when parts of the California coast experienced flooding. Recently set high tide records were also broken in Santa Barbara and La Jolla, according to preliminary data. King tides are the highest predicted tide of the year and are above the highest water level reached at high tide on an average day. While they typically occur once or twice a year in most coastal areas, they are happening more frequently in some as sea levels rise. These king tides are being strengthened by the strongest El Niño weather pattern on record. Image source, Getty Images Storm systems are also expected to impact the US through the Thanksgiving holiday - one affecting the Pacific Northwest and another the eastern part of the country, the US National Weather Service said. On the east coast, a developing Nor'easter storm is expected to bring heavy rain, strong winds, and coastal flooding. Major impacts are expected to begin on Wednesday across the southeast, and move northward Wednesday night and on Thursday, the NWS said. "Those along the eastern seaboard can expect gusty, possibly damaging winds, heavy rain, and major coastal and marine impacts," it added. The developing storm system could bring about one to four inches rain along America's east coast and a peak of four to six inches in coastal North Carolina. The US department store chain Macy's has postponed its 100th annual Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York, which was due to take place on Thursday, until Saturday because of severe weather - the first time this has happened. The stormy weather on both coasts could disrupt travel for millions of people. The American Automobile Association (AAA) has predicted that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles (80km) from home for Thanksgiving between Tuesday 24 November and Monday 30 November. Officials have also issued a flood warning along Vancouver's coastline in Canada's most western province of British Columbia. Residents were urged to take "immediate action to protect life and property", a weather warning from Environment and Climate Change Canada said. King tides were expected to potentially increase already high water levels there too.
