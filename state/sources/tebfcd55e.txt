It follows months of speculation over the Welsh government's intentions, after an expert group warned "a step change" was needed in efforts to eradicate the disease. While farming leaders have long called for tougher badger controls, a petition against culling has attracted more than 28,000 signatures on the Senedd website in recent weeks. The Welsh government said it would take "a new and pragmatic approach to TB eradication that is evidence-based and science-led". But the problem - which can be devastating for affected farms - is concentrated, with higher incidence levels in west Wales and along the border with England. There were 10,090 cows slaughtered following TB testing in the 12 months to June 2026. Under the previous Labour-run Welsh government, a target was set for Wales to completely eradicate bovine TB by 2041. But before May's Senedd election an expert group set up to advise ministers , external warned that goal would "not be met", and called for urgent action and more funding. Image source, Reuters Image caption, Cattle are slaughtered if they are found to be carriers of bovine TB The Bovine TB Eradication Programme Board's latest recommendations were published on Tuesday, alongside the government's response. On badgers, ministers said they were "not pursuing a mass, non-selective cull". However further research is proposed of the role played by badgers and deer in spreading the disease via new population surveys and fieldwork. This would then lead to developing "targeted and proportionate" interventions which could include trapping and testing badgers before vaccinating or euthanising them, "where the evidence supports this". Localised trapping and culling was trialled in Wales on nine farms between 2017 and 2024, but the cost involved drew criticism after hundreds of thousands of pounds was spent on killing a small number of badgers. Officials told a briefing for journalists that "selective removal" of badgers was a possibility in future, but "[we] cannot give you a timeline". Image source, Matthew Horwood/Getty Images Image caption, In July, the Senedd backed a Reform UK motion calling on the Welsh Government to consider targeted wildlife control, including badger culling Changes to cattle testing were also being proposed by the government, to help spot infection earlier. They will work with the farming industry to explore "safe ways" of moving cattle out of TB restricted herds, to help businesses continue to operate. Farms experiencing persistent issues and under so-called enhanced management will be offered more choice in how their situation is handled. There is talk of a new advisory service for farmers and vets - and moves to provide better, more up-to-date data on the latest disease risk. Image caption, For farming families "a TB breakdown can mean months or years of restrictions, uncertainty and difficult choices", said agriculture minister Llyr Gruffydd Cabinet Minister for Rural Resilience and Sustainability, Llyr Gruffydd MS, said bovine TB remained "one of the most serious animal health challenges facing Wales". "We will not stand by while farmers face uncertainty and anxiety, nor will we pursue a mass non-selective cull," he added. "Instead, we will be taking evidence-based and science-led action, incorporating the three pillars of cattle, people and wildlife. "Farmers and the public will rightly judge us on whether we prevent disease and reduce its impact, even when the decisions are difficult." Ian Rickman, president of Farmers' Union of Wales, said it was "positive to note that targeted and proportionate action will now be taken on TB in wildlife where evidence supports this approach". "With the loss of over 92,000 cattle and the percentage of herds under Bovine TB having remained stubbornly consistent for the past ten years, this is now the opportunity to intensify the co-working between industry, the veterinary profession, academics and government in order to deliver these recommendations without delay and further explore the wider proposals set out by the Programme Board," he added.
