The Netherlands Food and Consumer Product Safety Authority (NVWA) said a man flying from Mexico to Hong Kong via the airport was arrested on Saturday after the Vallarta mud turtles were found by customs officers. The endangered species, thought to be the smallest freshwater turtles in the world with a maximum length of about 10cm (4in), were wrapped in duct tape to prevent them from moving. The NVWA said they were "presumably stolen" from the wild in their native Mexico. It said most of the turtles were found in a box with a toy car, while the others were in a closed sock inside a suitcase. The arrested man remains in custody and an investigation is under way. Meanwhile, the turtles are receiving care at a "specialised sanctuary", the NVWA said. In 2025, the US NGO Turtle Island estimated that no more than 300 Vallarta mud turtles existed in the wild. They are listed as critically endangered on a list published by the International Union for Conservation of Nature. According to environmental news outlet Mongabay, a Vallarta mud turtle can sell for $10,000 (£7,500) or more on the black market in Asia. Other cases of live turtles being smuggled to Hong Kong have been reported this year. In October, customs authorities in Hong Kong were reported to have seized eight turtles , external that were thought to be endangered. The reptiles had an estimated market value of about $54,000. Three months prior, Hong Kong customs detected two separate smuggling cases , external . Some 42 live turtles, with a total estimated market value of $210,000 were seized.
