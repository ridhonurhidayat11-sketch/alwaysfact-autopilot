A Falcon 9 rocket lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base's Launch Complex-4E at 7:23 a.m. ET (1223 GMT; 4:23 a.m. local time in California). It was the sixth National Reconnaissance Office launch (NROL) this year to deliver a payload, or group of payloads, to low Earth orbit , all which have been carried out by SpaceX 's workhorse Falcon 9. The launch marks another step in the NRO's effort to expand its intelligence-gathering capabilities through a proliferated network of spacecraft designed to provide more frequent and timely surveillance of locations around the globe. A SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket launches the NROL-136 mission for the U.S. National Reconnaissance Office from California on Nov. 22, 2026. (Image credit: SpaceX) About two minutes after liftoff, the Falcon 9's first-stage booster separated from the rocket's upper stage, which continued carrying the classified payloads onward to orbit. The booster then reversed course and returned to Vandenberg, touching down at Landing Zone 4 approximately seven and a half minutes after launch. Saturday's launch was the 14th flight for this particular Falcon 9 booster, dubbed B1097, which previously launched NASA 's Sentinel-6B ocean-monitoring satellite, NROL-172, USSF-259 for the U.S. Space Force , and eight Starlink missions.
