Facebook Linkedin Threads Whatsapp Reddit Email Page link International Jaguar Day has been marked every Nov. 29 since 2018, to raise awareness of the threats faced by the Americas’ biggest cats and the efforts to protect them. Jaguars ( Panthera onca ) were once found across some 19 million square kilometers (7.3 million square miles), from what is today the southwestern U.S. to central Argentina. But their range has shrunk by more than half, and today spans about 9 million km² (3.5 million mi²). Most jaguars live in a stronghold ranging from the Amazon Rainforest and stretching into the Pantanal wetlands, the Chaco dry forest and the Llanos grasslands of Colombia and Venezuela. However, recent sightings and reports from outside these core areas highlight that many jaguars are still holding on at the edges of their historical territory. For example, in Brazil’s eastern Atlantic Forest and dry Caatinga, fewer than 250 adult jaguars survive, and researchers have begun banking their genes in an effort to save them through cloning . At the northern edge of the species’ range, a male nicknamed Cinco showed up on camera footage in southern Arizona state in late 2025, becoming the fifth jaguar recorded there in 15 years. Meanwhile, in Honduras’s Sierra del Merendón, a mountain range in the country’s northwest, camera traps recorded a young male at an altitude of about 2,200 meters (7,200 feet) for the first time in a decade. “The fact that they’re able to travel through these high elevation areas also shows how resilient they are,” Allison Devlin, who directs the jaguar program at the wildcat conservation NGO Panthera, told Mongabay’s Spoorthy Raman. The Merendón mountains form a corridor between Honduras and Guatemala, allowing wildlife to move between the two countries. “It reaffirms that there are dispersed populations in the northwestern part of the country,” said Marcio Martinez, a coordinator at Honduras’s National Institute of Forest Conservation. Also in Honduras, authorities raided the home of a businessman in Olancho, in the east, and seized a female jaguar estimated to be about a year old, along with a capuchin monkey, two parrots and a python. The jaguar is thought to have been captured in the Mosquitia forests, a stronghold for the species. The young cat now lives at a rehabilitation center run by the Honduran armed forces and may be returned to a protected area if considered fit enough.
