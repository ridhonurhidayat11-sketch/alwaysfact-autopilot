Kober (NASA/Catholic University of America) NASA Hubble Mission Team Goddard Space Flight Center Nov 25, 2026 Article An expanse of stars gleams in a rainbow of colors in these NASA Hubble Space Telescope images of the Milky Way. The images focus on an area of sky toward the galactic bulge, a densely packed, spherical region of stars that surrounds the center of a galaxy’s disk. These Hubble observations are part of a survey that will help researchers better interpret upcoming Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope observations as they seek to characterize the stars and search for exoplanets in this busy region of our galaxy. Astronomers study the Milky Way’s galactic bulge because it holds nearly a quarter of the galaxy’s stellar mass, and its proximity allows them to examine the region in greater detail. Although we can see the glow from these crowded spheres of stars in other galaxies, they are too jam-packed and far away to resolve individual stars. By studying the Milky Way’s galactic bulge, researchers hope to find new planets and better understand how galaxies form, evolve, and interact over billions of years. Kober (NASA/Catholic University of America) Hubble views the universe primarily in ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light, while Roman collects data in near-infrared light and some visible light. Because researchers wish to compare Hubble’s observations to future Roman observations, Hubble looked at the region in wavelengths that overlap with Roman’s areas of sensitivity, the red end of the visible spectrum and near-infrared wavelengths. Hubble’s high resolution allows its instruments to collect data on the brightness, ages, composition, and motion of stars over time.
