Her parents worked at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Maryland, and discussions about happenings at the agency continued even when they came home from the office. “I remember a poignant dialogue we had after the space shuttle Challenger disaster,” Knight recalled. “However, we talked just as much about the engaging work my parents were doing inside Goddard’s gates.” Her father Lyle Knight was a mechanical engineer, while her mother Julia Knight worked the bulk of her career in human resources. They brought their daughter on center for Take Your Child to Work Day and other events, and over time she became hooked. “I saw the chambers where spacecraft and instruments were being tested, as well as other facilities used to help further space exploration. Above all, I witnessed the passion and collaborative spirit everyone had at Goddard,” Knight said. “As a child, I found it incredibly fascinating.” Ashley Knight (right) on a visit to NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center in Greenbelt, Md., while her parents were employees. Photo courtesy of Ashley Knight It all came full circle in late August when, after growing up around NASA and later working several contractor roles for the agency, Knight and a number of her colleagues in the Goddard Office of the Chief Financial Officer were sworn in as federal employees as part of a workforce directive to strengthen the agency’s technical core competencies by bringing mission-critical positions into the civil service. She began her Goddard career more than two decades ago following graduation from the University of Maryland in nearby College Park. As the job search began after commencement, one organization inevitably stood out, along with one particular mission. When she came across a contractor job opening at Goddard in support of the Hubble Space Telescope team, Knight jumped at the opportunity. “Hubble was doing amazing things, and I always admired how they fixed the faulty mirror,” she said, referring to how astronauts and team members corrected an early spherical aberration during the observatory’s first servicing mission. “It was exciting to become involved with such a flagship mission, especially coming right out of college.” As a project support analyst, Knight assisted engineers with test plans, data gathering, and hardware procurement for Hubble’s fifth and final shuttle servicing mission. Knight stands in front of space shuttle Atlantis, which was used for the Hubble Space Telescope’s fifth and final shuttle servicing mission, while she was a project support analyst for the Hubble team.
