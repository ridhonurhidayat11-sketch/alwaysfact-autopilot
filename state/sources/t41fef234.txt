The BepiColombo mission — a joint effort of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency ( JAXA ) — will arrive in orbit around Mercury today (Nov. 21), more than eight years after leaving its home planet. The big moment will come after an orbit-insertion burn, which is scheduled to begin today at 1:24 p.m. EST (1824 GMT) and last for 37 minutes. BepiColombo consists of two main spacecraft: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (MMO), also known as Mio. The duo launched — along with a power and propulsion element called the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) — aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana in October 2018 , beginning a long and looping trip through the inner solar system. Here's what BepiColombo can do Mercury is a difficult planet to reach because it's so close to the sun . Spacecraft approaching the solar system's innermost planet are accelerated to high velocities by the immense gravitational pull of our star and must shed a great deal of kinetic energy to be captured by Mercury's much weaker tug. So the BepiColombo team designed a trajectory that featured one flyby of Earth , two of Venus and six of Mercury over the course of about seven years. That flight time was increased by a year , however, after the mission suffered an issue with its solar panels, which reduced the power available for the MTM's electric thrusters.
