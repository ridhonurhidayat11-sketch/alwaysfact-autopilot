The Fisher's estuarine moth has been spotted 111 times at Abbotts Hall in Great Wigborough, near Colchester, in 2026. Dr Zoe Ringwood, of Essex Wildlife Trust, said work to boost the highly threatened species' population had been an "important success story". The charity has pledged to continue surveying new and existing plots on the Essex coast. Fisher's estuarine moths can be recognised by their pale gold-brown wing colour with light and dark spots, and are naturally found in low-lying areas of natural grassland which are vulnerable to erosion. They are reliant on rare hog's fennel for their survival, but the plant has been affected by rising sea levels. To protect the species, Essex Wildlife Trust planted hog's fennel at Abbotts Hall in 2005 and introduced moth eggs five years later. Image source, Essex Wildlife Trust Image caption, There is now a self-sustaining population of the moth at Abbotts Hall, according to Essex Wildlife Trust More than 100 of the scarce insect were recorded for the first time in 2025, before the record was broken again in 2026. The charity said it was confident there was now a self-sustaining population at Abbotts Hall. "[It is] a reflection of how taking evidence-based conservation action can achieve positive results for a threatened Essex species," said Ringwood, its senior conservation evidence manager. Hog's fennel has also been planted at nature reserves in Fingringhoe, Wrabness and nearby Tollesbury. Do you have a story suggestion for Essex? Contact us below.
