"The Inner Light"'s life in 25 minutes is a strong contender, as are the courtroom grandstanding in "The Measure of a Man" and his insistence (under extreme torture) that "there are four lights!" in "Chain of Command". But for this writer's money, it's hard to look past a nine-minute sequence in "Star Trek: First Contact", which turns 30 this weekend. With the Borg overrunning the newly launched USS Enterprise-E, Picard — who has long-standing beef with the Collective, following his assimilation in classic "The Next Generation" story " The Best of Both Worlds " — orders his crew to fight the cybernetic nasties hand-to-hand if required, defying their pleas to activate the ship's self-destruct system and retreat. He even calls Worf — not a man renowned for dodging a fight — a coward, prompting the Klingon to respond that, "If you were any other man, I would kill you where you stand." Ouch. But following some impassioned talk about making the Borg "pay for what they've done", he's finally talked down from his rage by 21st-century stowaway Lily Sloane (Alfre Woodard). She points out that his Moby-Dick-esque obsession is on the verge of going full Ahab, and he eventually sees sense, issuing the order to abandon the Enterprise and destroy the hive. And just as the franchise's zenith, "The Wrath of Khan", is built around Kirk and Khan's decades-old feud, "First Contact" is precision engineered to get the most out of Jean-Luc.
