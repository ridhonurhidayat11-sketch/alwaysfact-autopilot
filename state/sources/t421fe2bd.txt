Photograph: Stefani Reynolds/Bloomberg via Getty Images US Senate Senate committee rejects Trump nominee to lead National Science Foundation Committee chair Bill Cassidy, a Republican, said Jim O’Neill ‘did not do enough to stand up for science’ in previous posts Melody Schreiber Wed 18 Nov 2026 12.46 EST Last modified on Wed 18 Nov 2026 12.48 EST Share Prefer the Guardian on Google Jim O’Neill will not undergo a confirmation hearing on Wednesday to head he National Science Foundation (NSF) because he “did not do enough to stand up for science” in his previous position, said Bill Cassidy, a Republican from Louisiana who is chair of the Senate committee on health, education, labor and pensions. This ends O’Neill’s run for the top US scientific organization, an embattled independent federal agency that has seen major budget cuts and grant freezes under the Trump administration . The next NSF nominee will face a Democratic-led Senate. White House withdraws nomination of Heidi Overton to lead FDA Read more O’Neill served as acting director of the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) from August 2025 to February 2026. In that role, he failed to push back against “irresponsible decisions and statements from Secretary Kennedy”, Cassidy said in opening remarks before the committee on Wednesday, hours before O’Neill was slated to appear. “His role in the firing of Susan Monarez raised further concerns about the independent judgment he would bring to NSF,” Cassidy added, saying that several members of the committee had concerns about those lapses. Cassidy said that he likes O’Neill personally, “but this time requires leadership and fortitude in standing up for what is right”. O’Neill also served as deputy secretary of the US Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) from June 2025 to February 2026, a time during which HHS slashed recommendations for routine childhood immunizations. The White House did not pull O’Neill’s nomination. But Cassidy noted that the White House did yank the nomination of Heidi Overton for commissioner of the US Food and Drug Administration, saying she lacked the votes to pass confirmation – which Cassidy said was “pretty odd”. “I think she would’ve had the votes,” Cassidy said.
